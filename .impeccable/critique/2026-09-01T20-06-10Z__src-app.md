## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment.** The spine of the compare page is authored for this product and nobody else's: teal-A / sienna-B column tints, per-row winner marks, "differs but unrankable" rows, a lens that re-scores wins, and a deal-breaker checklist that rewrites the verdict sentence in place. The wrapping around that spine is template: a "Two products. / One answer." hero with an accent-coloured second line under a kicker, three equal category cards, a four-column "attributes tracked" stats block, seven FAQ cards restating the answer, and picture-frame placeholder glyphs where product photos should be. Copy is mostly human but the machine seams show ("has does not fold", "1 spec wins", "LG leads ... but LG saves $400"). Would a design director believe AI made this? Yes for the home page and the lower half of the compare page; no for the decision panel and the tables.

## Overall Impression

The decision layer is the best thing on the site and it arrives too late, wrapped in too much. On desktop a shopper gets a real, cited answer about 870 px down; on a phone it is 1.7 screens down, after two brand cards, and the page itself is wider than the phone. The single biggest opportunity: make the answer the hero, delete everything that repeats it, and fix the mobile width. The type, palette and table craft are already good enough to carry that.

## What's Working

1. **The decision layer** (`src/components/DecisionPanel.tsx`, `src/lib/decision.ts`): a sentence with the numbers behind it, and a deal-breaker tick that visibly flips the pick ("Sony is out: does not fold"). It is honest when it cannot rank ("4 of 14 rankable"). This is the differentiator the brief asked for.
2. **Table craft on desktop**: tinted A/B columns, winner marks, dimmed identical rows, hide-identical with a status line ("11 of 14 attributes differ"), sticky product header. Baymard's top comparison-table findings are met on desktop.
3. **Type and palette**: Archivo plus a mono face for numbers, teal and sienna coded as sides rather than good/bad, and dark-mode tokens that hold up.

## Priority Issues

1. **[P0] The site is wider than a phone.** At 390 px the home page renders 642 px wide, the compare page 568 px, the category page 607 px. VS cards put product B's name off-screen, the sticky compare header shows Bose only, and the 4th lens chip is clipped with no cue. 42 of 53 interactive targets are under 44 px (chips 31 px, checkboxes 15 px, the Bose / Both / Sony switch 26 px).
   - Why it matters: half of comparison-shopping traffic is mobile, and the page's core promise (two products side by side) is physically broken there.
   - Fix: `grid-template-columns: var(--label-col) minmax(0,1fr) minmax(0,1fr)` at `src/app/globals.css:214`; `min-w-0` on the VsCard root and `minmax(0,1fr)` on the home and category card grids; a right-edge fade on the lens row; 44 px minimum targets on chips, checkboxes and the side switch.
   - Suggested command: `/adapt`

2. **[P1] Thin lenses answer with confidence they have not earned.** "Sound quality" and "Calls & work" cite 1 of 1 rankable spec, usually weight, and headline "Buy the Sony" on a 3 g difference.
   - Why it matters: this is the moment of trust; one visibly silly verdict discredits every honest one.
   - Fix in `buildAnswer` (`src/lib/decision.ts`): when a lens has fewer than 2 rankable rows, headline "Too close to call on sound quality from spec sheets; only weight ranks", never promote weight as evidence for sound, and show "1 of 4 rankable" next to the lens chip.
   - Suggested command: `/clarify`

3. **[P1] The answer is buried and then repeated.** Above the decision panel sit H1, verdict line, SEO boilerplate, and two full product cards (cards nested inside a card); below it a second win-summary section (`src/views/compare-matchup.tsx:352`) duplicates the bar the panel already renders, and "Which one", "Best for each use" and seven FAQ cards restate it again.
   - Why it matters: the peak moment lands 870 px down on desktop, the ending is the weakest section, and a shopper who has decided has nowhere to go.
   - Fix: replace the two product cards with 72 px product strips (mark, name, price, wins, Swap) directly above the panel; delete the duplicate win summary; move descriptions and pros into "Which one should you buy"; end the page with a real exit (retailer link, price check, or share-this-verdict) instead of FAQ.
   - Suggested command: `/distill`, then `/arrange`

4. **[P1] Muted text fails contrast everywhere.** `--ink-3: #85897f` (`src/app/globals.css:20`) is 3.3:1 on the page ground and is used for the deal-breaker hint, eyebrows, "row" links and the dimmed identical rows. The sticky column headers and the "Spec leader" badge are 10.5 px.
   - Why it matters: low-vision shoppers cannot read the very rows the table asks them to compare, and it is a single-token fix.
   - Fix: darken the light-mode token to `#6c7067` (4.6:1 on `#f5f5f1`, 5.1:1 on white; `#686c63` if it must also pass on the `#ecece6` fill), and raise the column headers and badge to 12 px.
   - Suggested command: `/audit`

5. **[P2] Machine seams in the copy.** "Check first: Sony WH-1000XM5 has does not fold" (caveat template in `src/lib/decision.ts`); "LG leads ... but LG saves $400" because `src/components/VsCard.tsx:64-69` uses brand names for both sides of same-brand matchups; "1 spec wins"; citations cut mid-word at 46 characters (`clip` in `src/lib/decision.ts:121`); the same boilerplate description on every compare card and page.
   - Why it matters: each seam tells the shopper a machine wrote the verdict, which is the opposite of the "tie-breaker you trust" position.
   - Fix: sentence templates per caveat kind ("does not fold" becomes "the Sony does not fold"), model names when brands match, pluralisation, clip at a word boundary, and page-specific descriptions.
   - Suggested command: `/clarify`

## Minor Observations

- Scroll-spy root margin (`src/components/SpecTables.tsx:114`) selects the last group after a bottom jump: clicking "Connectivity" highlighted "Ownership".
- The Swap `<details>` menu (`src/views/compare-matchup.tsx:188`) stays open under the search dialog; no Esc or outside-click close.
- `.p-mark { display: grid }` at `src/app/globals.css:446` is unlayered and overrides Tailwind's `hidden` / `sm:hidden`, doubling marks on category rows and showing the desktop mark inside the mobile sticky header.
- Headphones, air purifiers and credit cards fall through to the picture-frame default glyph in `src/components/ProductMark.tsx`, so the marks read as broken images.
- Category `firstMatchup` picks alphabetically, producing "Compare vs LG" on five of eight TVs; the "ranked" 1 to 8 is just price order.
- "What gets compared" on the compare page is a four-column metrics-grid filler section; the same attribute counts already appear in the hero chips.
- The compare index renders 105 cards on an 8,745 px page with no filter, and every TV card repeats the same boilerplate sentence.
- Header search (`src/components/SiteHeader.tsx:52`) lists compare entries first and slices to 8, so "sony" returns eight matchups and zero products.
- Header uses a `color-mix` background with blur; blurred ghosts of content show through as smudges behind the nav.
- The finance category card on the home page promises "Credit Cards · Loans · Investment Apps" when only cards exist.
- The lens uses `replaceState`, so the Back button does not undo a lens change.

## Questions to Consider

- If the answer is the product, why does a shopper scroll past 1,300 px of brand copy to reach it? What if the sentence were the hero and the product cards were 72 px strips?
- Should a lens be allowed to speak with one rankable datum? Would "we can't rank sound quality from spec sheets" build more trust than "buy the Sony"?
- What does "done" look like? A shopper who has decided has nowhere to go; the page ends in FAQ and a footer.
- When 10 of 14 rows are "differs but unrankable", is a win count the right spine, or would "the three things that actually differ" beat the table for most shoppers?
